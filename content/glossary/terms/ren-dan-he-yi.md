Rendanheyi (人单合一) is Haier's management model, developed under Zhang Ruimin beginning in 2005, for linking a person's work and rewards directly to value created for an identified user. It is a model of organizational accountability; the definition alone does not establish that any particular company or agent currently satisfies it.

人 (*rén*) names the person or maker, 单 (*dān*) the particular user's need and value—not simply a sales-order number—and 合一 (*hé yī*) their joining. Haier summarizes the incentive principle as 薪酬来自用户, “salary comes from users.” The claim concerns value-derived compensation and accountability, not an assertion that every user's payment directly becomes a worker's legal wage. In the model, the **value-added pay component** depends on delivered user value; activity, tenure and managerial approval alone cannot substitute for that value.

To make the principle operative, Haier organizes work as [xiaowei micro-enterprises](xiaowei.md), with [decision, personnel and distribution rights](sanquan.md), [value-adjustment commitments](duidu.md), user-facing accounts and [user-derived pay](ren-dan-chou.md). Rights, obligations and economic exposure belong together: a relabeled department on an unchanged supervisory budget does not become a micro-enterprise.

The organization changed over time. The source account describes the 2013–2014 reorganization as removing roughly ten thousand middle-management positions and forming several thousand micro-enterprises, followed by a significant 2019 shift toward [chain groups](lianqun.md). Those are historical claims about Haier's reported transformation, **not** a timeless unit count or an independently verified result in this glossary. The vocabulary and composition of units differ across editions.

Rendanheyi is not merely a flatter hierarchy, employee engagement, permission for side projects or agile/lean execution. Those describe organization shape, attitude or work practices; this model changes the relationship among user demand, authority, risk and value allocation. Haier has promoted it beyond its original enterprise, including GE Appliances after its 2016 acquisition. Its applicability outside that context remains contested: an organization retaining veto power over a purportedly autonomous unit has not implemented the full economic relation described by the model.
