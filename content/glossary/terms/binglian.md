**Binglian** (并联, *parallel wiring*) is the electrical-circuit analogy used in Rendanheyi to describe how [chain-group](lianqun.md) members should be accountable for the **same user outcome**. Its contrast is 串联 (*series wiring*), in which each unit is answerable primarily to the next unit in the sequence.

## Accountability, not simultaneous work

In a series arrangement, a design unit may satisfy manufacturing's specification and be paid even though the final user receives little value. Each handoff introduces a possible internal payer, local performance measure and negotiation. A defective final outcome can coexist with several locally satisfied contracts.

In the model's parallel arrangement, design, manufacturing and market units are all meant to answer to one end-user result, through the [chain-group contract](lianqun-heyue.md). An intermediate unit is not meant to release payment simply by accepting a handoff. The practical work may still run **in strict sequence**; what is parallel is the accountability, not the physical execution. The wiring analogy is a design explanation, not proof that a deployed group's contracts, accounts or customer receiving actually implement it.

## Not the same as

Parallel wiring is not concurrent execution, a matrix reporting structure, or a cross-functional team whose members remain funded and appointed by their departments. A lianqun that retains separate internal payments or managerial override has not satisfied the idealized parallel-accountability claim merely by using the name.
